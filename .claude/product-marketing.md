# Dopazon — product marketing context

Read by the `cro` and `analytics` skills before they ask questions.

**What it is.** A parody of an online megastore where everything costs $0.
You browse, add to cart and check out, and get the dopamine of shopping
without spending money. Tagline: "Satisfy the urge. Keep the money."

**Who it is for.** People who impulse-shop online and want the hit without
the bill; people trying a no-buy month; people who enjoy the joke.

**Core promise.** "You did not spend $X." The account page leads with a
running total of money not spent. That number is the product.

**Money.** Amazon affiliate links (tag `dopazon-20`) on the product page,
shown when a user says they would buy the real item. No ads, no payments.

**Primary conversions, in order.**
1. First add-to-cart (the moment the joke lands).
2. First free checkout (records savings).
3. Sign up (keeps the savings total across devices).
4. Return visit / daily drops streak.
5. Affiliate click-out.

**Current reality (2026-10).** Organic visitors arrive with zero marketing,
but almost nobody acts: 9 signups ever, 0 orders, 0 feedback, 0 confessions.
The priority is converting the visitors who already come, not more traffic.

**Voice.** Deadpan Amazon parody. Confident, a little absurd, never mean.
"Free delivery (fake)", "Verified Fake Purchase", "Returns: keep it".

**Hard constraints.**
- No real brand names or logos in product names or photos.
- Reviews and ratings on the site are jokes. Never emit structured data
  (Review, AggregateRating, Offer with a real price) that presents them as
  real — it breaks search engine policy and misleads.
- Never imply real goods are shipped or real money is taken.
